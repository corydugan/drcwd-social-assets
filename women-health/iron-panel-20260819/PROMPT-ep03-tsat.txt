Then a list writes itself on, numbered 01 to 11: Serum ferritin, Serum iron, Transferrin saturation, Total iron-binding capacity, Transferrin, Haemoglobin, Soluble transferrin receptor, Mean corpuscular volume, Hepcidin, Zinc protoporphyrin, C-reactive protein. A thin accent rectangle then draws itself around row 03, Transferrin saturation, and the other ten dim back to about a quarter opacity. The narration is speaking continuously across this whole beat, starting as the first words appear, so the list is being written while the voice is talking. Do not leave a pause between the question and the list.

NAME. The panel clears. State plainly what transferrin saturation measures: the proportion of the blood's iron carrying sites that currently have iron in them.

CARRIER. A single transferrin molecule enters frame and travels along a vessel. Draw it as a clean, ruled, mechanical object, not a blob, and keep it moving across the whole beat. Label it TRANSFERRIN, THE CARRIER. Say that the liver builds it and that it is what moves iron through the blood.

SEATS. The carrier settles to centre frame and its two binding sites are revealed on it, as two empty rings. Two, exactly two, and the picture must count the way the biochemistry counts.

FERRIC. A loose iron ion approaches the carrier labelled Fe2+, is oxidised as it arrives, and becomes Fe3+ before it drops into one of the two rings and fills it. Show the change of state as a visible moment. This is the beat that earns the detail: iron must be in its ferric form before transferrin can carry it.

FLEET. The one carrier multiplies into a field of carriers, eight or so, all travelling, some with both sites filled, some with one, most with none. They should be moving continuously, not parked.

COUNT. Every binding site in that field lifts out of the carriers and lines up as a single row of cells, occupied ones solid, empty ones outlined. The row assembles by animation, cell by cell.

SHARE. The occupied portion of that row separates from the empty portion, so the proportion reads at a glance. Label it SATURATION, THE SHARE THAT IS TAKEN. Do not print a percentage.

MEANING. Close the argument: a low saturation is one of the two readings a clinician uses to call iron deficiency. State that it reads how full the transport is, not how much iron there is.

SIGNATURE. Everything clears to the field colour. An equilateral triangle draws itself on as an outline, fills solid, then the wordmark drCWDugan writes on beside it, and a thin accent rule draws underneath. Nothing else. This is the only place the brand name appears.

Typography:

Line art only. Strokes at 4 to 6 pixels. No gradients, no drop shadows, no 3D, no glow, no bevels. Exactly one element per beat may be filled solid, and that element is the point of the beat. Everything else is a hairline.

Keep 35 to 40 percent of the frame as clear field. This is a vertical phone format and it should feel spacious, not packed.

Minimum readable size is 32 pixels on this 1080 wide canvas. Anything smaller is decoration and should be cut rather than shrunk.

## Text layout, and this one has bitten this project before

Do not size any label, callout or box by estimating its width from character count multiplied by a per character constant. That under measures real glyph advance and the longest strings overflow. Let the text lay out its own box, so overflow is structurally impossible regardless of string length. Verify every text box at several time points and confirm nothing extends past its container.

## The science, and please keep it exactly this

Transferrin is a protein made in the liver that transports iron through the blood.

Each transferrin molecule binds up to two atoms of ferric iron, Fe3+. Iron must be oxidised from the ferrous form, Fe2+, to the ferric form before transferrin can bind it.

Transferrin saturation is the proportion of the available iron binding sites that are occupied. It is a measure of how full the transport is, not a measure of how much iron the body holds.

A low transferrin saturation is one of the two readings used to diagnose iron deficiency, the other being serum ferritin.

Do not state any threshold, any percentage, or any reference range. Do not claim what a normal value is.
