## What to change

Give every line at least its spoken duration plus one second of headroom, so the voice finishes and the picture has a beat to breathe before the next line arrives. Using the numbers above that means these minimum slot lengths, in order: 5.2, 6.8, 7.8, 6.4, 7.9, 3.6, 7.1, 9.3, 8.5, 7.4, 8.5, 6.7. That totals about eighty-five seconds of narration, so the finished piece runs roughly ninety seconds including the signature beat.

Stretch the ANIMATION inside each beat to fill its new length. Do not simply hold a finished frame for longer while the voice catches up. If a beat gets three more seconds, the movement in it should take three more seconds: the carrier travels further, the ions arrive more slowly, the row of binding sites assembles at a gentler rate. A held still frame with a voice over it is the exact failure this whole rebuild is meant to remove.

Keep the crossfades and the reveal timings proportional to their new beats rather than fixed, so nothing snaps.

## The rule going forward, for every episode in this series

Time every beat against the SPOKEN length of its narration, never the read length. A useful working figure is one hundred and forty words per minute, which is about 2.4 words per second, then add one second of headroom per beat. A beat should never be shorter than its own line takes to say aloud.

There must never be a stretch where the narration has stopped and the animation is still running, and never a stretch where the picture is idle while the narration runs. Both of those are failures. Every beat carries motion and words together, from the first second to the last.
