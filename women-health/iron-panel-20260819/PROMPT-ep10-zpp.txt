## The science, and please keep it exactly this

Haem is the part of haemoglobin that holds iron, and your body builds it one step at a time.

The last step drops a single metal atom into a ring. When iron is there to be had, that metal is iron.

When it is not, zinc goes in instead, and the result is zinc protoporphyrin. The wrong molecule builds up, and counting it is the test.

### Two things you must NOT do

Do not claim this reading is specific to iron deficiency. It is not. Lead exposure raises it as well, by restricting the iron reaching that same final step, and any picture implying one cause only is wrong. You do not have to explain lead. Just do not build the episode on a claim of one cause.

Do not draw iron and zinc as two greys. The whole beat is that the viewer SEES the swap happen, so give the two metals a visible difference: the accent for one and the primary white for the other.

Print no threshold, no reference range and no percentage. Do not say what a normal value is.

## Timing, and this is the instruction that matters most

Time every beat against how long its narration takes to say ALOUD, never against how long it takes to read silently. Speech runs about 2.4 words per second. Take the spoken length of the line and add 0.8 seconds of headroom, and that is the beat.

Three earlier rounds of this series failed on exactly this: beats were sized for reading, so the voiceover overran every one of them and lapped into the next.

Aim for about 100 words of narration in total across the whole piece, which lands at roughly 63 seconds including a closing signature beat. Do not go past 110 words. A rebuild of episode 03 ran to about 200 words, which takes roughly 85 seconds to say aloud, and the piece had to be stretched to 92 seconds to hold it. Cut the words, do not stretch the picture.

Pace the animation genuinely at 63 seconds. Do not build a longer piece and play it fast.

There must never be a stretch where the narration has stopped and the animation is still running, and never a stretch where the picture is idle while the narration runs. Every beat carries motion for its whole length. If a beat has no movement in it, the beat is wrong. Do not reach the end of a movement and then hold a finished frame while the timeline catches up.

An audit of the hand-built series measured an average of 19.0 seconds of silence inside a 45 second video, which is 42 per cent of it, made of a fixed gap after the intro hook and a long tail where the voice had finished and the animation had not. That is the failure this section exists to stop.

Build it the way a motion piece is built, not as a slideshow: things travel, doors open, quantities fill. Nothing should simply fade in, sit still, and fade out.

## Visual system, hard constraints

Field colour #0B0B0C on every frame. This is a dark piece and the field is never white and never purple.

Use only these:
- field #0B0B0C
- primary text #FFFFFF
- secondary text #E4E5E7
- muted text and labels #C8CACE
- hairlines and rules #2A2A2D
- the accent, for the ONE thing that matters in each beat #D8CFE7
- the eyebrow and small caps labels #8A6FB8

Purple is an accent and an ink. Never a background fill, never a large area of colour. Neutrals carry more than ninety percent of every frame.

Typography:
- display and headlines: DM Serif Display
- body, labels and small caps: DM Sans
- any figure, unit or evidence string: IBM Plex Sans

Line art only, strokes 4 to 6 pixels. No gradients, no drop shadows, no 3D, no glow, no bevels. Exactly one element per beat may be filled solid, and that element is the point of the beat. Everything else is a hairline.

Organs and cells are drawn loose and organic. Machinery, carriers and containers are drawn ruled and mechanical. That contrast is deliberate and it carries meaning.

Keep 35 to 40 percent of the frame as clear field. This is a vertical phone format and it should feel spacious, not packed.

Minimum readable size is 32 pixels on this 1080 wide canvas. Anything smaller is decoration and should be cut rather than shrunk.

## Text layout, and this one has bitten this project before

Do not size any label, callout or box by estimating its width from character count multiplied by a per character constant. That under measures real glyph advance and long strings overflow. Let the text lay out its own box so overflow is structurally impossible regardless of string length. Verify every text box at several time points and confirm nothing extends past its container.
