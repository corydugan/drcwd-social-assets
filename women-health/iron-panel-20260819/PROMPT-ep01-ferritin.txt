## The science, and please keep it exactly this

Ferritin is the protein your body stores iron in, inside its cells.

A small amount of it leaks into the blood, and that is what a ferritin blood test measures. So the reading is a read on the SIZE OF THE STORE rather than on the iron currently in use.

Haemoglobin is the iron already at work, inside red blood cells, carrying oxygen.

When iron runs short, the store is what empties first. Haemoglobin only falls once the store is gone. That ordering is the whole point of the episode: it is why a full blood count can come back normal in someone who is already iron deficient.

### Two things you must NOT do

Do not say ferritin tells you exactly how much iron is stored. It does not. Ferritin is an acute phase reactant and rises with inflammation, so a normal ferritin in an inflamed person does not settle the question. The word "exactly" is the first thing a haematologist would attack and the episode does not need it.

Do not open the inflammation topic here at all. It has its own episode later in the series. Mentioning it in half a sentence is worse than leaving it out.

Print no threshold, no reference range and no percentage. Do not say what a normal value is.
