--- CAPTION ---
We gave 14 women a single intravenous iron infusion and 12 a placebo. Four weeks later the iron group was running more efficiently and reporting less fatigue.

That is the IRONWOMAN trial, and the conclusion in the paper is one sentence: intravenous iron therapy corrects iron status and improves both exercise economy and fatigue scores.

Twenty-six iron-deficient, non-anaemic, recreationally active women. Double-blind, randomised, assessed at baseline, day 4 and week 4.

What moved.

1. Serum ferritin, serum iron and transferrin saturation all corrected.
2. Running economy improved from baseline to week 4, and was better than placebo at week 4.
3. Fatigue scores improved on iron and not on placebo.

Running economy is the one I keep returning to. It means the same pace costs you less oxygen. For somebody who is tired and training around it, that is the part they actually feel.

The individual responses are the other half of the story. Same 1000 mg infusion, same four weeks, and the rise in ferritin ran from 73 to 508 ug/L. Every woman on iron went up. The size of that rise was not the same in any two of them, which is the argument for measuring rather than assuming.

So if you screen a workforce or a squad for iron deficiency, this trial tells you what to put on the sheet. Measure ferritin. Measure fatigue. Retest, because the average response is nobody's actual response.

Twenty-six participants is small, and they were recreationally active rather than a working cohort.

Educational content, not individual medical advice.

If you screen for iron deficiency, what do you track afterwards?
